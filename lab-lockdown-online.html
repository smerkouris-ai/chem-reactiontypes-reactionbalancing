Lab Lockdown

A chemical reactions escape room

00:00Not started

A late-night experiment set off the lab’s security system, and your team is sealed inside. The building’s computer will only release the doors if you prove you understand chemical reactions.

There are four locks, opened in order. Work out every answer as a team, then press the button to test it. A wrong answer won’t lock you out, but the computer won’t tell you which part was wrong.

  • Everyone on the team should be able to explain every answer.
  • You may use your notes and a periodic table.
  • After three wrong tries on a lock, the computer gives you a hint.

  1. Lock 1: The Sorting Terminal

    The terminal flashes five reactions from the night’s experiments. Classify each one. The decoder turns each type into a letter.

  2. Lock 2: The Balance Vault

    The vault opens only when all four equations are balanced with the lowest whole-number coefficients. An empty box counts as 1.

    A H2 + O2 → H2O
    B Al + O2 → Al2O3
    C C3H8 + O2 → CO2 + H2O
    D KClO3 → KCl + O2

    Count every atom on both sides before you test it.

    The vault clicks open. Atoms balanced on both sides.

  3. Lock 3: The Reactivity Gate

  4. Lock 4: The Exit Door

    The final door asks one question: where does the mass go? Answer each problem in whole grams.

    Problem 1. Baking soda and vinegar react in an open beaker. Bubbles of CO2 escape into the air. The beaker and contents had a mass of 312 g before and 309 g after. How many grams of gas escaped?

    g

    Problem 2. The same reaction runs inside a sealed zip-top bag that starts at 215 g. How many grams of mass are lost by the end?

    g

    Problem 3. In 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO, 4.8 g of magnesium reacts completely with 3.2 g of oxygen. What mass of magnesium oxide forms?

    g

    Explain it. In one sentence, why do Problems 1 and 2 give different answers for the same reaction?

    Mass conserved. The exit door unlocks.
